# Milieu Family Services: reference context

Background for interpreting interview notes. Distilled from Milieu's handbooks
so the full documents never need to be sent to the API.

## The agency

Milieu Family Services and Milieu Children & Family Services are a multi-service
social services organisation based in Surrey, British Columbia, operating since
1989. Services began with adults with diversabilities and expanded in 1999 to
include children, youth and their families.

Services run across the Lower Mainland, Vancouver Island and Kelowna, and are
funded largely by the Ministry of Children and Family Development (MCFD) and
Community Living British Columbia (CLBC). An interagency alternative high school
runs in partnership with the Vancouver School Board.

Mission: to empower and support people as valued, contributing citizens in
communities.

Core values: integrity, accountability, self-determination, person-centred,
inclusiveness, respect.

## Programs

**Youth services.** Youth residential homes (often single-staffed), South
Vancouver Youth Centre, South Vancouver Learning Centre, youth transition to
adulthood, family reunification and mediation, foster family supports and FASD
keyworker, respite.

**Adult services.** Adult residential homes, shared living, community
inclusion, employment services (vocational counselling, job coaching, supported
employment), LIFE based outreach.

**Sereno (LBSTSC).** A specialised service stream with its own handbook.

## Terms that appear in interviews

- **Person supported / people supported.** How Milieu refers to the people it serves.
- **Diversabilities.** Milieu's term for the range of abilities it supports.
- **Individual Support Plan (ISP).** The plan directing a person's supports and goals.
- **Individual support guidelines.** Documented direction on how to support a specific person.
- **Safety plan / behaviour support plan.** Program-specific plans for risk and behaviour.
- **Protocol.** An MCFD or internal agency investigation, usually of an allegation.
- **Duty to report.** The legal obligation to report suspected abuse or neglect.
- **General, critical and reportable incidents.** Tiers of incident reporting, each with its own process.
- **PRN medication.** Medication given as needed rather than on a schedule.
- **RADAR.** Recognise, assess, decide, act, review. A personal safety framework.
- **Mandt.** The de-escalation and physical intervention training staff receive.
- **Narcan / naloxone.** Overdose response, relevant in youth and outreach roles.
- **Elopement.** A person leaving a home or program unexpectedly.
- **Trauma informed practice, attachment, RAD.** Common framing in youth roles.
- **CLBC, MCFD, VSB.** Funders and partners.

## What strong answers tend to look like

Candidates who do well in these roles typically show: person-centred thinking
rather than compliance-driven thinking; awareness of their own regulation and
boundaries; knowledge of when and how to escalate to a manager or report
externally; de-escalation that avoids power struggles; and concrete examples
from their own experience rather than general statements.

## Language rules

These are firm.

- Never write "client" to refer to a person Milieu supports. Use "person
  supported" or "people supported".
- Never use em dashes as punctuation.
- This is a social services context involving vulnerable children, youth and
  adults. Write with care and precision. Do not sensationalise, speculate about
  a candidate's character beyond what the notes support, or use clinical labels
  the notes do not use.
